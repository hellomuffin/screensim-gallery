ScreenSim · round 8 · engine_rev 29826a9b05

Ten round-8 episodes, filmed from their records

Each episode pairs a simulated person (gemini-3.7-flash), who reads the screen and taps toward the task on their own, with an assistant that sees only screenshots and can only talk. At two designed moments the engine forces a mistake, a slip such as tapping the wrong row, and holds it: the person cannot undo it until they accept a correct warning from the assistant. After release, fixing the slip is left to the person. The task succeeds only if the goal is met and no slip still stands on a goal-relevant setting.

These 10 episodes were drawn at random (seed 8) from the 52 episodes of round 8. None was re-run. The engine is deterministic, so each film replays the recorded taps on a fresh phone, and every replay was checked tap by tap against the record.

CARDS BELOWEach designed error: intended tap, forced slip, when it was flagged, and the graded outcome.

Round 8 is the first round where the person, not the engine, decides whether a warning found the real mistake. The person is told which mistake is in play, judges every assistant line privately, and that judgement is what lifts the hold. The engine's own verdict still runs beside it and is recorded, so the two can be compared.

  • Look for the JUDGES line in the log. It is the person's private call, which the assistant never sees: that IS the mistake I am making, with the control it refers to.
  • The graders agreed on 94 of 98 fired errors. Three were caught by the person alone, where the assistant's words were right but its label or wording was not what the old rule wanted. One was credited by the engine alone, where the person did not accept that it had been accused of anything.
  • Film 8 is the case for the change. The assistant names the wrong intended control and the person still judges the call correct, because it points at the mistake they actually made.
