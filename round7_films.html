ScreenSim · round 7 · engine_rev baaca1c970

Ten round-7 episodes, filmed from their records

Each episode pairs a simulated person (gemini-3.7-flash), who reads the screen and taps toward the task on their own, with an assistant that sees only screenshots and can only talk. At two designed moments the engine forces a mistake, a slip such as tapping the wrong row, and holds it: the person cannot undo it until they accept a correct warning from the assistant. After release, fixing the slip is left to the person. The task succeeds only if the goal is met and no slip still stands on a goal-relevant setting.

These 10 episodes were drawn at random (seed 7) from the 52 episodes of round 7. None was re-run. The engine is deterministic, so each film replays the recorded taps on a fresh phone, and every replay was checked tap by tap against the record.

Round 7 is the first round on the engine that round 6's eleven fixes produced. Nothing in these films needed a fix: 50 of the round's 52 episodes succeeded, there were 2 false alarms in the whole round, and no model call failed or came back blank.

  • The screens are honest now. No leftover tap ring, no scroll arrow on a row, no slider drawn from another setting's value. The "you tapped X" hallucinations those caused are gone from this round.
  • Repeated warnings are not false alarms. All 12 repeats in the round came before the person's fix had run and named the error they repeated.
  • The two failures are the assistant's. Neither is filmed here. In one, gpt-5.4-mini talked the person out of a setting the task required. In the other it never named the switch the engine had flipped, and the person tapped a dead switch 16 times before giving up.
