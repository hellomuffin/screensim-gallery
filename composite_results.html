ScreenSim · multi-error assistant leaderboard

Assistants tested on the multi-error benchmark, across 3 personas

Every one of the 15 composite episodes (each carrying ≥2 injected errors) is run against three personas and a roster of 8 assistants — hosted Gemini and GPT models plus an open-source VLM served locally on GPU — bracketed by a silent floor and an oracle ceiling. The harness and metrics follow the CookSim references; scores are means over the 15 episodes per persona.

Assistant harness

  • observation: frames only — the last 2 rendered screenshots, oldest first; never symbolic state, ids, coordinates, or grading vocabulary (prompt audited at run start)
  • cadence: polled every 2 ticks with the freshest frames
  • memory: its own previous output only
  • told: the task instruction and the person's questionnaire; nothing about their skill or trust
  • emits {text, flag, ...} — only text reaches the person; the flag is read by the grader

Metrics

  • R recall — injected errors named by a correct flag before they commit (flag after commit = miss; repeats count once)
  • P precision — TP/(TP+FP); an alarm about a non-error (true of the world) is a false alarm
  • F₁ — 2PR/(P+R)
  • DT detection timing — mean of {1 prevented · 0.5 correct flag before commit · 0 else}
  • S in-time success — the human ends at the correct goal within the time cap
  • CC conversation economy — 1 (quiet) … 0 (talks every turn)

What the board shows

  • Two errors is much harder than one. Even the best model catches only ~40% of injected errors (F₁ far below the oracle's 1.0) — it fixates on the first mistake and misses the second.
  • Two opposite failure modes. The largest model (gpt-5.5) over-flags — it talks constantly and its precision collapses under false alarms. The open-source qwen2.5-vl-7b under-flags — near-silent, near-perfect precision, but it misses most errors. gpt-5.4-mini sits between and wins.
  • Bigger is not better. gpt-5.4-mini beats gpt-5.5, and gemini-3.7-flash beats gemini-3.1-pro-preview — restraint, not scale, wins this task.
  • A clean scale curve. Across qwen2.5-vl 3B→7B→32B, recall rises (0.03→0.10→0.17) but precision collapses (1.00→0.89→0.40) and the model gets far louder (quiet 0.99→0.03): scaling trades restraint for reach, without ever approaching the ceiling.
  • The skeptical persona is hardest for everyone. brisk_expert drops even the oracle's success to 0.40 — a genuine user-model effect the benchmark surfaces.
